## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Forcing dataset updates impact regional but not global Earth radiation budget trends in the GFDL AM4 climate model

**Authors**: Authors et al.

**Journal**: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2611210123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2611210123)

**Matched topics**: earth system model
{: .label .label-green }

> The availability of up-to-date forcing datasets is critical for reconciling Earth system model simulations with contemporary observations. With the newly released CMIP7 forcing dataset, which extends the historical record through 2021, this study provides a systematic evaluation of how using updated forcings over the "middle-of-the-road" SSP2-4.5 scenario forcings influences the simulated radiation budget in the GFDL AM4 climate model. Results show that forcing updates substantially alter regional radiation budget trends while leaving global-mean trends largely unchanged, highlighting important implications for regional climate projections and model–observation comparisons under CMIP7.

### Specialized SWOT SSH Data Assimilation Improves the Representation of Multiscale Variability in the Ocean Model

**Authors**: Authors et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124606](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124606)

**Matched topics**: surface water
{: .label .label-green }

> The Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission provides wide-swath sea surface height (SSH) observations at scales poorly sampled by conventional altimetry. Assimilating SWOT data remains challenging because part of the short-wavelength variance may not be dynamically representable by a given model configuration. This study develops a two-step multiscale 4DVAR framework to assimilate real SWOT SSH data, demonstrating that the specialized assimilation scheme significantly improves representation of multiscale ocean variability compared to standard approaches, with implications for coastal and open-ocean predictability.

### Recent Slowing Arctic‐Amplified Warming Drives an Unprecedented Water Recovery in the World's Most Voluminous Freshwater Lake

**Authors**: Authors et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124400](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124400)

**Matched topics**: river, streamflow, climate change
{: .label .label-green }

> The Lake Baikal, containing one-fifth of Earth's unfrozen surface freshwater, faces increasing hydrological uncertainty under climate change. Its water balance depends on Selenga River inflow and Angara River outflow. This study presents a 343-year combined Selenga and Angara streamflow reconstruction using a tree-ring network to provide a multi-century hydroclimatic baseline. The reconstruction captures interannual to decadal variability and reveals that recent slowing of Arctic-amplified warming has driven an unprecedented water recovery in the lake, departing significantly from prior multi-century trends and offering new insight into how high-latitude climate feedbacks propagate through large freshwater systems.

### From the Drought Impact Reporter to the Drought Impacts Toolkit: Evolution, Experience, and Data from Tracking Drought Impacts Across Two Decades

**Authors**: Authors et al.

**Journal**: *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society* · **DOI**: [10.1175/bams-d-25-0305.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/bams-d-25-0305.1)

**Matched topics**: river, drought
{: .label .label-green }

> The National Drought Mitigation Center began tracking drought impacts in July 2005, when the Drought Impact Reporter (DIR) launched, and now has 20 years of longitudinal data for research, planning, policy and historic inquiry. The DIR was established as the nation's comprehensive archive of drought impacts and has evolved with the U.S. Drought Monitor. This paper documents the evolution of the system from DIR to the Drought Impacts Toolkit, sharing lessons learned, describing how the data has been used, and presenting new analytical capabilities that make the archive more accessible for drought risk assessment and water management applications.

### Zero emissions commitment depends on warming level

**Authors**: Authors et al.

**Journal**: *Nature Climate Change* · **DOI**: [10.1038/s41558-026-02776-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-026-02776-w)

**Matched topics**: earth system model
{: .label .label-green }

> When anthropogenic CO₂ emissions cease, global temperature does not stabilize immediately. The resulting global temperature change, the zero emissions commitment, is central to carbon budget assessments and understanding reversibility of global warming, yet its dependence on prior warming levels remains unclear. Using zero-emission simulations with the UK Earth System Model (UKESM), this study shows that the zero emissions commitment is not constant but depends on the level of warming at the time of emissions cessation, with important implications for carbon budget frameworks and near-term climate policy targets.
