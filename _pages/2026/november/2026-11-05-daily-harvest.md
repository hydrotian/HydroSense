### Saltwater Intrusion Seasonality Under Climate Change: Interacting Effects of River Discharge, Sea‐Level Rise, and Bathymetry

**Authors**: Dongxiao Yin, Courtney Kay Harris, Duc Tran, Mahesh R. Tapas, Venkataraman Lakshmi, Michael A. O'Driscoll, Stephen Moysey

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl122717](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122717)

**Matched topics**: river, seasonal, climate change, estuary
{: .label .label-green }

> Saltwater intrusion is a growing concern under climate change. Although previous studies emphasize intensified intrusion during low‐flow periods, changes in its seasonality remain understudied. We combine long‐term observations with coupled hydrologic–hydrodynamic modeling to examine how river discharge and sea‐level rise (SLR) affect intrusion seasonality in Pamlico River Estuary. We find that intensified discharge seasonality, with lower low flows and higher high flows, amplifies intrusion seasonality by driving landward salt‐front expansion during low‐flow periods and oceanward retreat during high‐flow periods, increasing its seasonal migration range. Moderate SLR increases intrusion magnitude while maintaining high seasonal variability. Under higher SLR, however, landward topography constrains low‐flow expansion and reduces seasonal migration. These findings provide a mechanistic framework for understanding saltwater intrusion seasonality under climate change: river discharge and SLR determine how strongly the salt front tends to migrate, while its position relative to local topography determines whether that migration remains unconstrained or limited.

### Calibrating the Geomorphic Clock: Remote Sensing of Sediment Storage and Transit Timescales in Alluvial River Corridors

**Authors**: Huck Rees, Vamsi Ganti, Evan Greenberg, Gen K. Li, Kelly K. Caylor

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124018)

**Matched topics**: river, flood
{: .label .label-green }

> Constraining sediment and organic carbon storage duration in river corridors is essential for understanding terrestrial carbon cycling and interpreting the sedimentary record. However, existing methods rely on geochemical dating or theoretical models tailored to idealized meandering rivers, limiting their broader application. Here, we present a remote sensing framework using four decades of Landsat imagery and an area‐based mobility metric to quantify decadal‐scale channel mobility across diverse river planforms. We extrapolate these observations to millennial‐scale sediment storage and transit times using a bounded random‐walk model informed by remotely derived channel‐belt widths. Modeled storage and transit times reproduce independent geochemical benchmarks within a factor of three across four morphologically diverse rivers. River planform and channel mobility timescales independently govern sediment transit: multithreaded reaches transmit stored sediment faster than meandering reaches, and slower floodplain reworking prolongs transit. Our results provide a scalable foundation for quantifying fluvial sediment and carbon dynamics globally.

### Historical Groundwater Memory Predicts Site‐Specific Persistence Horizons Across Germany

**Authors**: Funing Ma, Zhenxue Dai, Fangfei Cai

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl127221](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl127221)

**Matched topics**: seasonal
{: .label .label-green }

> Groundwater levels retain memory of antecedent hydrologic conditions, but whether this memory defines a persistent timescale of later groundwater behavior remains unclear. We analyzed weekly records from 3207 wells across Germany, estimating groundwater memory during 1991–2005 and persistence during 2013–2022. Historical autocorrelation‐implied timescales strongly predicted later persistence horizons (Spearman ρ = 0.851; 95% confidence interval, 0.819–0.877), with a median absolute difference of 2 weeks. A well's own historical memory was much more closely related to later persistence than memory reassigned among hydrogeologically and spatially similar wells, indicating strong site specificity. Historical hydroclimatic–groundwater coupling was also associated with departures from the autocorrelation‐based expectation, and this relation persisted after removal of seasonal and trend components. These site‐specific timescales inform persistence‐based forecasting and provide a temporal perspective for groundwater resilience assessment and climate adaptation planning.
