### How AI is changing research

- **[Claude, find me a planet: amateur astronomers are using AI with mixed results](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03790-w)** (Nature, 2026-11-20) — Citizen scientists are now deploying Claude as an AI research assistant to sift through telescope data for exoplanet candidates, with the story highlighting both genuine discovery wins and common failure modes. For earth scientists, this is a direct template: the same workflow — prompt an LLM to reason over large observational datasets, flag anomalies, and triage candidates — applies readily to automated feature extraction from satellite imagery (flood extents, river morphology change, soil moisture anomalies). The barrier used to be building the pipeline; now it's mostly writing the prompt and validating the outputs.

- **[The most misunderstood graph in AI just got a major update](https://www.technologyreview.com/?p=1149399)** (MIT Technology Review, 2026-11-19) — MIT Technology Review reports that the neural scaling laws graph — the foundation for decisions about how to allocate training compute across model size and data — has been revised with new empirical evidence. For research teams using AI in earth science, this matters practically: it informs whether to invest in a larger foundation model or more domain-specific fine-tuning data, and helps calibrate expectations for model performance as a function of compute budget.
