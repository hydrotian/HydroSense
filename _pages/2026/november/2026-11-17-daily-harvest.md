## Today's Highlights

A single highly relevant paper emerged from today's harvest: a new high-resolution neodymium isotope reconstruction of North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW) extent through the intensification of Northern Hemisphere Glaciation (~2.73 Mya). The study finds that NADW expanded dramatically during this major climate transition, while glacial-interglacial variability in NADW extent was surprisingly muted in both the Late Pliocene and Early Pleistocene. These findings suggest that the warm Pliocene climate state operated with a fundamentally different deep-ocean circulation regime, with direct implications for understanding AMOC stability under future warming.

## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Expansion of North Atlantic Deep Water during Northern Hemisphere Glaciation

**Authors**: Friso de Graaf, David Thornalley, David J. Wilson, Zarina Hewett, Yair Rosenthal, Stella Woodard et al.

**Journal**: *Nature Communications* · **DOI**: [10.1038/s41467-026-79566-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79566-7)

**Matched topics**: river, Pleistocene
{: .label .label-green }

![Figure](https://media.springernature.com/m685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-79566-7/MediaObjects/41467_2026_79566_Fig1_HTML.png)

> Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) is a major driver of the Earth's climate, moving heat from the Southern Hemisphere to the Northern Hemisphere. Neither foraminiferal carbon isotope records nor modelling studies agree on the spatial extent of North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW), the northern deep limb of the AMOC, over the intensification of Northern Hemisphere Glaciation (iNHG, ~2.73 million years ago). Here we present high-resolution neodymium isotope (ε Nd) records from a depth transect in the equatorial Atlantic over the iNHG. We find a persistent latitudinal and depth gradient in neodymium isotope values in the deep Atlantic during the Late Pliocene, absent in the Early Pleistocene, indicating an expansion of NADW extent over the iNHG. In contrast, our reconstruction suggests very little glacial-interglacial variability in NADW extent during both the Late Pliocene and Early Pleistocene. These results suggest deep-water circulation was substantially different during a warm climate state, with implications for the role of NADW formation during major climate transitions and its future stability.
