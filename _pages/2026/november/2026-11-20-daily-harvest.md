## Today's Highlights

A GRL study couples remote sensing with a Coupled Vegetation–Water Dynamics model to reveal that ecosystem resilience under drought is controlled by a trade-off between environmental sensitivity and ecological regulation—not recovery alone—with characteristic collapse times ranging from 3.3 years in the La Plata Basin to 4.6 years in the Amazon. A companion Nature Climate Change paper shows that concurrent atmospheric phenomena (the co-occurrence of multiple large-scale drivers) amplify precipitation extremes beyond what individual drivers produce, with the effect strengthening under anthropogenic climate change and carrying direct implications for flood risk estimation and hydrologic design.

## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Nonlinear Dynamics of Ecosystem Resilience Under Drought Disturbances

**Authors**: Junyi Liu, Xiang Zhang, Aminjon Gulakhmadov, Rabindra Kumar Panda, Mahlatse Kganyago, Veber Costa et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl125010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl125010)

**Matched topics**: drought, climate change
{: .label .label-green }

> Drought is increasingly challenging ecosystem stability under climate change, yet the mechanisms underlying ecosystem resilience remain poorly understood. Here, we integrated remote sensing observations with a Coupled Vegetation‐Water Dynamics (CVWD) model to quantify vegetation‐water responses to drought across five major ecological regions. The model successfully reproduced observed drought trajectories and revealed that resilience is jointly governed by environmental sensitivity, ecological regulation, and recovery capacity. Ecological regulation weakened with increasing drought severity, whereas recovery capacity increased with recovery duration. A strong trade‐off between environmental sensitivity and ecological regulation emerged across ecosystems, indicating that resilience cannot be explained by recovery alone. Long‐term stochastic simulations revealed substantial regional differences in persistence, with characteristic collapse times ranging from 3.29 years in the La Plata Basin to 4.64 years in the Amazon rainforest. These findings identify key controls on ecosystem resilience and provide a scalable framework for assessing drought vulnerability under climate change.
