## AI for Science

### How AI is changing research

- **[He Wants AI Benchmarking To Be a Science, Not a Dark Art](https://www.quantamagazine.org/he-wants-ai-benchmarking-to-be-a-science-not-a-dark-art-20261016/)** (Quanta Magazine, 2026-10-16) — Argues that current AI benchmarking is ad-hoc and irreproducible, and lays out a framework for treating evaluation as a rigorous scientific practice. As AI forecasting models become standard tools in hydrology and earth-system modeling, the same evaluation rigor matters: benchmark datasets must be designed to prevent leakage, metrics must capture the failure modes users care about, and model-ranking claims must be reproducible — the same discipline that is now being demanded in the broader ML community.

### Cross-discipline sparks

- **[History's long-secret codes are being cracked by amateurs using AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03338-y)** (Nature, 2026-10-16) — Amateur cryptographers are using LLMs and computer-vision models to decipher historical manuscripts that stymied professionals for decades. The same pipeline — OCR-style transcription + LLM interpretation + domain-ontology grounding — could help earth scientists mine pre-instrumental flood diaries, drought chronicles, and water-management ledgers held in archives across Asia, Europe, and the Americas. Extending hydrological time series from 100 to 500+ years would transform flood-frequency analysis and paleohydrologic reconstruction; the barrier has always been the labor of reading archaic scripts at scale, and that barrier is now largely dissolved.
