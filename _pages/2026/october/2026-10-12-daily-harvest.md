## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Hydropeaking Catalyzes Redox Reactions in Impacted Aquifers: A First Field Evidence

**Authors**: Gabriele Chiogna, Francesca Ziliotto, Mónica Basilio Hazas, Carla Feistner, Klara Hoebenreich, Mohammad Alqadi et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl125793](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl125793)

**Matched topics**: river, hydropower, surface water
{: .label .label-green }

> Hydropeaking indicates the sudden and frequent fluctuation of river stage caused by the operation of hydropower plants. This river alteration affects numerous physical, chemical, biological, and ecohydrological processes in surface water bodies. The impact of hydropeaking on groundwater hydraulics has been investigated only recently through modeling and experimental studies at multiple spatial scales. This study presents new hydrological and geochemical surface water and groundwater data providing the first evidence of the impact of hydropeaking on groundwater hydrogeochemistry. To interpret these observations, we propose a conceptual model where the rapid vertical oscillation of the groundwater table acts as a pressure-driven "geochemical pump" impacting redox conditions in the aquifer. This work has important implications for better understanding of groundwater biogeochemistry as well as for nutrient and carbon cycles.

### Extensive Glacier Loss in the Coterminous United States

**Authors**: Andrew G. Fountain, Bryce Glenn, Christopher McNeil, Brian Menounos

**Journal**: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2600941123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2600941123)

**Matched topics**: hydrology
{: .label .label-green }

> Glaciers populate the mountain ranges of the western coterminous United States and despite their importance to alpine hydrology, their area and volume change have never been comprehensively evaluated. Our study shows that since the mid-twentieth century about one-quarter of the glaciers have disappeared and the overall area and volume are reduced by one-third. The pattern of glacier shrinkage suggests most glaciers in the coterminous United States are largely maintained by local topographic influences due to their small size. These results improve our understanding of evolving high alpine hydrology and importance to ecosystem processes.

### Improved Assessment of Alaskan Arctic Coastal Wave Exposure Accounting for Landfast Ice

**Authors**: Martin Henke, Zhaoqing Yang, Ruth Branch

**Journal**: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2612829123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2612829123)

**Matched topics**: seasonal, coastal
{: .label .label-green }

> Simulations of the ocean wave climate are critical for assessment of coastal processes and hazards in observation sparse regions such as the Arctic. While the multidecade wind and ice datasets necessary for driving long-term wave simulations have proven reliable for such efforts, the presence of landfast ice along shorelines at scales not represented by these data has been a persistent uncertainty impeding accurate analysis of impacts to Arctic coasts. Here, we explore this uncertainty by producing a historical high-resolution wave simulation and combining simulation outputs with landfast ice data for the Alaskan Arctic. The resulting dataset yields an improved assessment of coastal waves and identifies stark contrasts in the seasonal wave exposure of the Chukchi and Beaufort regions. Notably, analysis shows that absent supplemented landfast ice, coastal ice cover is broadly underestimated by 10 to 25 d, resulting in wave energy being overestimated by 5 to 10% in shallow depths nearshore. Notably, these figures can reach up to 70 d of underestimation for ice cover and 48% overestimation of wave energy in certain locations. The persistent overestimation and potential for extreme positive biases in coastal wave assessment highlight the need for representation of landfast ice in Arctic coastal analysis efforts and our analysis reveals the spatial and temporal biases for related research.

### Contribution of the Pacific Decadal Oscillation to Global Mean Sea Level Trends: Revisited

**Authors**: Yingjie Wang, Xianyao Chen

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124649](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124649)

**Matched topics**: runoff
{: .label .label-green }

> Three decades of satellite altimeter observations reveal a continued rise in global mean sea level (GMSL), with the rate of rise reflecting the combined effects of decadal variability and long-term warming. By decomposing 30-year running trends of reconstructed sea level over 1950–2019, we quantify the contribution associated with the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO) to the GMSL trend during the satellite altimetry era. Our results indicate that, over 1990–2019, the PDO-associated contribution to the GMSL trend is 0.45 ± 0.07 mm/yr. This contribution is mediated through steric sea level changes (approximately 0.09 ± 0.03 mm/yr) and ocean mass changes related to land precipitation, evaporation, and runoff (approximately 0.34 ± 0.07 mm/yr). After removing the PDO-associated influence, the acceleration in 30-year GMSL trends decreases by about 70%. These findings underscore the importance of accounting for decadal sea level variability to detect the secular acceleration associated with anthropogenic warming.

### Meteorological Aspects of the Congo Basin and Western Equatorial Africa

**Authors**: Sharon E. Nicholson, Andreas H. Fink

**Journal**: *Reviews of Geophysics* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2025rg000923](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025rg000923)

**Matched topics**: seasonal
{: .label .label-green }

> This article focuses on the Congo basin and western equatorial Africa (WEA). Topics include atmospheric circulation, convective systems, the character of rainfall, cloud cover, moisture and evapotranspiration, and interannual variability. Some misunderstanding exists about the seasonal cycle. It has generally been described as bimodal, with rainy seasons in the boreal spring and autumn as the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) transits the region. This is true of only some central portions and some of the region has a unimodal seasonal cycle. More importantly, the ITCZ does not exist in this region. Intraseasonal variability is related to equatorial waves, the most important being convectively-coupled Kelvin waves and the Madden-Julian Oscillation. Main circulation features are the Tropical Easterly Jet, two mid-tropospheric African Easterly Jets, and low-level westerlies. A Walker cell exists over the region in some seasons. There is also evidence of a shallower, Walker-type cell in the Congo basin itself. The Congo basin is the site of the world's strongest convective activity, including the world's highest lightning frequency. Dominant are Mesoscale Convective Systems, which bring convective rainfall in the afternoon and stratiform rainfall at night. As for rainfall, there is little correlation on monthly or annual time scales between rainfall at various stations. The factors in interannual variability in the Congo basin are not well understood. In WEA sea-surface temperatures are important. El Niño-Southern Oscillation appears to have little influence, but the Indian Ocean dipole has some influence in the east. Other factors include changes in the Walker circulation, the African thermal lows, and the low-level westerlies.
