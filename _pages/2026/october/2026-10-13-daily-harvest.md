## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Decadal Sea Level Variability Along the U.S. East Coast: Roles of Extratropical Versus Tropical Climate Modes Over the North Atlantic Ocean

**Authors**: Yingli Zhu, Weiqing Han, Jianing Wang, Jian Zheng, D. L. Suhas, Aneesh C. Subramanian et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl125873](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl125873)

**Matched topics**: river, flood, coastal
{: .label .label-green }

> Coastal decadal sea level anomalies (SLAs) in the U.S. east coast (USEC) play a critical role in modulating coastal flooding frequency, yet relative influences of tropical versus extratropical climate modes over the North Atlantic remain unresolved. By integrating observations, simulations from simplified dynamical models and climate models, we show that remote forcings drive the most USEC decadal SLAs since 1950, with approximately half of this signal transmitted via westward-propagating Rossby waves. Notably, the tropical climate modes, the Atlantic Meridional Mode (AMM) and Atlantic Nino, exert comparable remote influences as the extratropical climate modes in the USEC. We identify a key mechanism that AMM-related wind and buoyancy forcings act constructively in the northwest Atlantic, amplifying decadal SLAs in the north USEC. Our findings reveal that tropical Atlantic variability is an important, yet previously underappreciated, driver of regional sea level, underscoring its critical role in decadal predictions and flood risk assessments.

### Seawall‐Induced Isolation Alters Hydrological‐Biogeochemical Conditions in Coastal Wetlands

**Authors**: Bingnan Zhao, Zhijun Dai, Xixing Liang, Wensheng Zhang, Fangbing Xu, Xuefei Mei, Sergio Fagherazzi

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl125301](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl125301)

**Matched topics**: estuary, coastal
{: .label .label-green }

> Coastal wetlands are increasingly impacted by tropical cyclones and storm surges. Seawalls and levees built to reduce these hazards disconnect wetlands from adjacent uplands, yet their hydroecological effects remain poorly understood. Using continuous hydrological observations in Chongming Island, Changjiang Estuary, China, we compared seaward and landward wetlands separated by a seawall. CTD sensor data, water quality measurements, and plot surveys revealed that seawall construction weakens tidal connectivity and reduces hydrological exchange landward. Seaward wetlands exhibit tidal-dominated dynamics with higher salinity and lower chlorophyll (CHL), chemical oxygen demand (COD), and total organic carbon (TOC), associated with presence of *Phragmites australis*. Interior wetlands show reduced salinity, higher values of CHL, COD and TOC, stronger rainfall-driven responses, and are characterized by more terrestrial vegetation species. These findings suggest that seawalls can substantially alter wetland hydrodynamics and ecosystem structure, highlighting the importance of incorporating hydrological connectivity into seawall design to sustain wetland functions.
