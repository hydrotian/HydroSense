## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### EMIT Reveals Elevated Calcite Signatures in Shorelines of Lake Powell and Lake Mead Exposed by Drought and Colorado River Management

**Authors**: Michael R. Fischella, Joel B. Sankey, Ray Kokaly, Amy East, Alan Kasprak, Joshua Caster et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl122195](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl122195)

**Matched topics**: river, reservoir, drought, seasonal
{: .label .label-green }

> Nearly half of Earth's seasonal freshwater storage variability occurs in human-managed reservoirs, resulting in large areas of reservoir-bed that are episodically inundated or subaerially exposed. Here we use hyperspectral data from NASA's Earth Surface Mineral Dust Source Investigation (EMIT) mission to map calcite mineral assemblages across exposed surfaces in two large U.S. reservoirs, Lake Mead and Lake Powell. Results show distinct patterns in the occurrence of carbonate, clay, and mica minerals. Reservoir-deposited sediments exhibited significantly higher calcite proportions than adjacent, lithologically similar, non-inundated terrestrial surfaces. These differences likely reflect hydrologically-driven calcite precipitation, wherein warm, clear, productive reservoir waters drive calcium carbonate out of solution. We also detect reductions in calcite proportions in areas of the shoreline disturbed by human recreation. EMIT's imaging capabilities enable rapid, high-resolution mineralogical inventories across vast areas where field sampling is infeasible. We find EMIT is a scalable tool for monitoring geochemical changes associated with large water level fluctuations.

### A Shift of Multidecadal Trend in Coastal Upwelling Off Southern Vietnam in the South China Sea Around 1999

**Authors**: Mai‐Han Ngo, Yi‐Chia Hsin, Chau‐Ron Wu

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl126805](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl126805)

**Matched topics**: coastal
{: .label .label-green }

> To explore multidecadal trends of the Vietnamese Southern Coastal Upwelling (SCU), we employed an SCU index (Southern Coastal Upwelling index [SCUI]), defined as the difference between basin-wide SST (T_ref) and local minimum SST (T_min). During 1982–2024, SCUI shifted markedly around 1999, weakening before 1999 (B99) and intensifying thereafter (A99). Empirical Orthogonal Function (EOF) analyses identify two key dynamical processes: (a) a persistent La Niña–like SST pattern accompanied by intensified Pacific trade winds, enhancing northern South China Sea (SCS) anomalous anticyclonic circulation and reducing SCS Throughflow; and (b) multidecadal variability in interhemispheric thermal contrast, resulting in cross-equatorial winds. Under their joint influence, the B99 weakening was dominated by a reduced southwesterly summer monsoon affecting T_min regulated by trade winds and cross-equatorial winds, while the A99 intensification was attributable to T_ref warming induced by enhanced northern SCS negative wind stress (WS) curl anomaly and weakened SCS Throughflow rather than enhanced local upwelling itself.
