## Land Surface Model Evaluation

Validation of land surface models in data-sparse arid regions remains challenging, and geodetic approaches offer a promising complement to traditional in-situ measurements. Mao et al. compare GNSS-derived vertical displacements against GRACE satellite retrievals and Noah-MP model predictions in the Heihe River Basin (northwestern China), finding that while network-mean agreement between GNSS and GRACE is moderate (r = 0.48), station-level agreement deteriorates in the basin interior. Notably, Noah interannual variations diverge from both geodetic sources, suggesting that the model's representation of arid-region hydrological processes — including groundwater dynamics and subsurface storage — needs improvement. The study highlights the potential of GNSS as an independent long-term constraint on terrestrial water storage variability and underscores the need for improved scale matching in multi-source hydrological monitoring.

### Scale-Dependent Agreement of Hydrological Loading Displacements from GNSS, GRACE, and a Land Surface Model in the Heihe River Basin

**Authors**: Jing-Zun Mao, Yue Gao, Jian-Min Bian, Jongmin Park, Yao-Hui Nan, Rong-Chang Zhao et al.

**Journal**: *Remote Sensing* · **DOI**: [10.3390/rs18213673](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18213673) · **Citations**: 0

**Matched topics**: land surface model
{: .label .label-green }

> Ground-based Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) vertical displacements provide an independent measure of hydrological loading, but their ability to resolve terrestrial water storage (TWS) variations in arid inland basins remains uncertain. This study compared vertical displacements from 15 GNSS stations within and around the Heihe River Basin with loading displacements derived from satellite-based GRACE and model-based Noah TWS anomalies. After removing non-hydrological signals from GNSS series, correlations among the network-mean vertical displacement for GNSS-GRACE, GNSS-Noah, and GRACE-Noah were 0.48, 0.49, and 0.74, respectively. Mean station-level correlations for the same comparisons were lower (0.38, 0.35, and 0.60, respectively), with weaker agreement within the basin interior. Time-series decomposition showed that GNSS captured inter-annual TWS variability more consistently than the seasonal cycle of TWS. GNSS generally peaks earlier than GRACE in the south but later in the north. Noah interannual variations diverged from both GNSS and GRACE, suggesting limitations in the model's representation of arid-region hydrological processes. Overall, GNSS provides an independent constraint on long-term TWS variability and a diagnostic of land surface model performance. These findings support multisource monitoring of arid-basin water storage while highlighting the need for improved scale matching and in situ validation.
