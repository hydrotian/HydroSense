## Today's Highlights

A Geophysical Research Letters study presents a unified modeling framework combining landscape evolution with inverse modeling of river profiles and thermochronologic data to reconstruct continuous rock uplift histories. Applied to south-central Germany over 160 million years, the method reveals high uplift rates from 160–80 Ma, a prolonged decline from 80–20 Ma, and renewed westward-intensifying uplift to present-day. This framework advances our understanding of the geodynamic controls that drive river network evolution and long-term landscape change, directly informing how fluvial systems respond to tectonic forcing over geological timescales.

## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Reconstruction of Rock Uplift in South‐Central Germany Since Late Mesozoic Times

**Authors**: Thomas Bernard, Alexander B. Neely, Christoph Glotzbach, Daniel Peifer, Yanqing Shi, Mirjam Schaller et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl125450](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl125450)

**Matched topics**: river, landscape evolution
{: .label .label-green }

> Rock uplift and denudation act as first‐order controls on geological processes that influence atmospheric and ocean composition, and landscape‐forming processes. However, estimating rock uplift rates remains challenging due to the temporal averaging of rates calculated from individual thermo‐ or geochronologic systems. Here we extract uplift rates in a unified modeling framework by combining landscape evolution with inverse modeling of river profiles and thermo‐geochronologic data. We reconstruct Late Mesozoic to present‐day rock uplift of south‐central Germany, using a data set comprising 17 detrital cosmogenic radionuclide samples and 30 thermochronologic bedrock samples. Inversions reveal high uplift rates from 160 to 80 Ma, followed by a decline from 80 to 20 Ma and renewal of uplift until present‐day. This current uplift event is characterized by increasing uplift toward the west across southern Germany. Our results provide a framework to resolve continuous uplift histories and quantify landscape evolution processes and geodynamic controls on Earth's surface.
