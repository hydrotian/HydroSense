### Atmospheric stabilization of the East Antarctic ice sheet under warming

**Authors**: Kyung-Sook Yun, Axel Timmermann

**Journal**: *Nature Climate Change* · **DOI**: [10.1038/s41558-026-02791-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-026-02791-x)

**Matched topics**: earth system model
{: .label .label-green }

![Figure](https://media.springernature.com/m685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41558-026-02791-x/MediaObjects/41558_2026_2791_Fig1_HTML.png)

> Most coupled Earth system models do not include fully interactive ice sheets, introducing uncertainty in future projections of polar climate and global sea level. To this end, we conduct 100,000-year-long simulations for different CO₂ conditions using the community Earth system model coupled to the bihemispheric Penn State University ice sheet–ice shelf model. Whereas global warming exceeding 2 °C above pre-industrial conditions and related subsurface ocean warming lead to the millennial-scale disintegration of the West Antarctic ice sheet, the East Antarctic ice sheet shows a much greater resilience, even at 10 °C or more of warming. The simulations reveal that a moister and cloudier atmosphere over Antarctica, with southward-shifted westerlies, increases snow accumulation and reduces shortwave radiation. For Greenland, a 6 °C threshold above pre-industrial levels is identified. Capturing the balance of ocean- and atmosphere-driven ablation and accumulation processes in coupled climate–ice-sheet models will improve projections of long-term sea-level rise.

### Seasonal Modulation of ENSO Teleconnections to Horn of Africa Rainfall

**Authors**: Charles Yusuph Ntigwaza, Wenjun Zhang, Teng Zhang, Feng Jiang, Hyacinth Nnamchi

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl123196](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl123196)

**Matched topics**: seasonal
{: .label .label-green }

> Rainfall over the Horn of Africa (HoA) exhibits pronounced interannual variability linked to the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO). However, the seasonal evolution of ENSO-driven rainfall anomalies is not adequately explained by the ENSO, particularly the Niño3.4 index. The results here show that nonlinear interactions between ENSO and both semiannual and annual migration of the Intertropical Convergence Zone generate a combination mode (C-Mode) that modulates the timing and amplitude of the HoA regional rainfall. The C-Mode reproduces the rainfall variability during both the October–November–December and March–April–May rainy seasons, and captures statistically significant spectral peaks at combination frequencies near 1 and 2 years⁻¹. Regression and reconstruction analyses indicate that the inclusion of the C-Mode substantially improves representation of the bimodal rainfall response. These findings highlight the key role of ENSO–seasonal cycle in driving HoA rainfall variability and provide a physically-based pathway for the region's improved seasonal prediction.
