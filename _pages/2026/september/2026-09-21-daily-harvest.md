## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### Compound drought–heat wave hazards are concentrated in US regions with multiple social vulnerabilities and agricultural dependence

**Authors**: Amitesh Sabut, Ashok Mishra, David Zilberman

**Journal**: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2607273123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2607273123)

**Matched topics**: drought
{: .label .label-green }

> Compound drought and heat wave (CDHW) events are becoming more frequent and intense across the contiguous United States, increasing risks to human health, ecosystems, agriculture, and energy production systems. However, their distribution across socioeconomically vulnerable communities remains largely unexplored at the national scale. In this study, we develop a CDHW metric by integrating weekly US Drought Monitor classifications with daily temperature extremes from 2000-2021, allowing consistent mapping of CDHW occurrences along county-level socioeconomic conditions. Our analysis reveals significant regional differences, with elevated CDHW occurrences concentrated in the South, Southeast, and western United States. Regions facing greater socioeconomic challenges, including poverty, high agricultural employment, and health-related vulnerabilities, experience notably higher CDHW risks. Greater spatial concentration of CDHW occurrences, as indicated by higher Gini coefficient values, occurs where multiple socioeconomic vulnerabilities intersect, such as in counties with large poor populations and older housing. These findings indicate that CDHW events are unevenly distributed across socially vulnerable communities, underscoring the need for targeted adaptation strategies to reduce climate risks for the most vulnerable communities.

### Subsurface‐Intensified Marine Heatwaves: A Hidden and Intense Warming Threat to Tropical Ocean Ecosystems

**Authors**: Wenbo He, Fuan Xiao, Chengyi Qu, Qilin Chunpi, Jun Zhao

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124321](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124321)

**Matched topics**: marine heatwave
{: .label .label-green }

> Understanding how the vertical structure of marine heatwaves (MHWs) influences ecosystems remains limited. Here we investigated subsurface‐intensified (Type‐1) and surface (Type‐2) MHWs in tropical oceans using reanalysis data sets. Type‐1 MHWs occur most frequently and are concentrated near the equator, with a threefold greater median vertically integrated cumulative anomaly (VICA) than Type‐2 MHWs. Although both types are primarily modulated by ENSO, their underlying mechanisms differ: Type‐1 MHWs are more closely linked to oceanic dynamics, whereas Type‐2 MHWs are strongly influenced by atmospheric heating. Critically, the annual mean VICA of Type‐1 MHWs increased significantly during 1993‐2020, driven by a deepening of the anomaly penetration depth rather than by intensification per unit depth. Ecologically, both types of MHWs reduce surface chlorophyll‐a; however, Type‐1 MHWs suppress deep chlorophyll maxima by exacerbating nutrient limitation, posing more severe vertical threats to phytoplankton biomass. These findings highlight the distinct nature and increasing risk of subsurface MHWs.
