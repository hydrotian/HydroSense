## Today's Highlights

A new remote sensing dataset (ECOCO3) combining co-located OCO-3 and ECOSTRESS observations reveals how well paired solar-induced fluorescence and thermal infrared data can resolve ecosystem water-use efficiency (WUE) at diurnal and seasonal scales. The study demonstrates that satellite observations can capture midday drought responses across FLUXNET sites spanning diverse vegetation types and climate zones, a key capability for constraining carbon–water feedbacks in land surface models. Sample size remains the primary limiting factor for distinguishing vegetation- and climate-driven WUE differences, pointing to opportunities for improvement as these missions continue.

## Top-Tier Journal Papers

### OCO‐3 Meets ECOSTRESS: Insights Into Ecosystem Diurnal Water‐Use Efficiency From Co‐Located Solar‐Induced Fluorescence and Thermal Observations

**Authors**: Zoe Amie Pierrat, Thomas P. Kurosu, Abhishek Chatterjee, Joshua B. Fisher, Maggie Johnson, Le Kuai et al.

**Journal**: *Geophysical Research Letters* · **DOI**: [10.1029/2026gl124105](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026gl124105)

**Matched topics**: drought, seasonal
{: .label .label-green }

> Terrestrial ecosystem regulation of carbon and water fluxes is critical for constraining climate–biosphere feedbacks but remains poorly quantified across space and time. Here, we evaluate whether co‐located observations from NASA's Orbiting Carbon Observatory‐3 (OCO‐3) and the ECOsystem Spaceborne Thermal Radiometer Experiment on Space Station (ECOSTRESS) can reproduce ecosystem water use efficiency (WUE) dynamics observed at FLUXNET sites across temporal scales, vegetation types, climates, and drought conditions. We created the ECOCO3 data set, which harmonizes OCO‐3 and ECOSTRESS observations in space and time. ECOCO3 captures broad seasonal and diurnal carbon and water flux patterns including midday drought responses. Sampling sensitivity analysis shows that ECOCO3 is primarily limited by available sample size for distinguishing vegetation and climate driven differences in WUE. Our findings highlight both the promise and limitations of remote sensing for resolving sub‐daily carbon–water coupling.
